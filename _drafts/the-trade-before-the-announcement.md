The pattern is documented. Nobody's doing anything about it.

On the morning of March 23, 2026, at 6:49 AM, oil futures trading volume spiked to nine times its normal level. About 6,200 contracts changed hands in sixty seconds. The typical volume for that time of day, averaged across the previous five sessions, was 700 contracts. $580 million in crude oil futures moved in a single minute.

Fifteen minutes later, President Trump posted on Truth Social that the United States had held talks with Iran and was halting planned strikes on Iranian energy infrastructure. Oil prices dropped sharply. The Dow Jones Industrial Average surged more than a thousand points. Whoever held those positions made an enormous amount of money.

That was March. By April 7th, traders had placed $950 million in oil futures bets in the hours before the ceasefire announcement. Same structure, same timing, same result.

Senators Warren and Whitehouse sent letters to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission requesting an investigation. Representative Torres described it as "potentially the largest instance of insider trading in history." Regulatory surveillance systems flagged the activity. Multiple news organizations reported on it.

And here we are in August.

## The Announcement Cycle

Since February 28th, when the US and Israel launched nearly 900 strikes against Iran in twelve hours, oil markets have been driven almost entirely by one variable: what Trump says next.

When Trump signals escalation, oil prices spike. When he hints at a deal, oil prices fall. The moves are substantial -- 4 to 8 percent in a session, sometimes more. The range this year has been roughly $80 to $111 a barrel, and the oscillation between those extremes traces almost exactly to a sequence of social media posts.

This is documented. It is not a fringe reading of the data.

Goldman Sachs estimated that Persian Gulf crude output is down 57 percent from pre-war levels. US crude exports soared 60 percent to nearly 6.5 million barrels per day -- a record high. The United States became the world's swing producer almost overnight. The Permian Basin hit a record 6.13 million barrels per day in April. Exxon and Chevron reported windfall second-quarter profits so large that Trump himself complained about them publicly, calling the companies out for making "too much money."

That complaint, made on the same day oil fell 5 percent on news of potential US-Iran talks, moved markets again.

The cycle runs in both directions.

## What the Data Actually Says

I want to be precise about what is and isn't established.

What's documented: the timing. Six thousand contracts in sixty seconds, nine times normal volume, fifteen minutes before a market-moving announcement. This happened repeatedly -- not once. Similar anomalies preceded Trump's "Liberation Day" tariff pause in April 2025 and US military action in Venezuela earlier in 2026. The pattern predates the Iran war and has appeared across different asset classes: stock index futures, oil futures, cryptocurrency.

What's not yet proven publicly: who placed the trades, whether they had advance knowledge, and whether the information asymmetry was created deliberately. The beneficial ownership of those March 22 and April 7 positions exists in records held by CME Group and Intercontinental Exchange. Those records require a subpoena to access. The agency with subpoena power is the CFTC. The CFTC answers to the executive branch.

The investigation is being spearheaded by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission -- which has been weakened under the Trump administration.

Draw your own conclusion.

## The Managed Band

Here is what I think is actually happening, stated plainly and with the acknowledgment that I cannot prove it:

The volatility is the product.

A war that killed Iran's Supreme Leader, closed the Strait of Hormuz, and drove US energy exports to record highs is obviously a real military event with real consequences. But the oscillation -- the specific cycle of threat, retraction, threat, retraction -- has a structural benefit that is entirely independent of any military objective.

When oil is above $115, it creates political pressure on domestic gas prices and threatens economic growth. When it drops below $80, the profit cycle for whoever is trading the volatility shrinks, and the incentive for domestic producers weakens. The band between those numbers -- roughly $80 to $100 -- keeps multiple sets of interests satisfied simultaneously.

US shale producers are making extraordinary returns. Defense contractors are operating at capacity. Domestic energy is at record output, reinforcing US geopolitical leverage with Europe and Asia, who are now buying American LNG and crude in volumes they never anticipated. And someone with fifteen minutes of advance notice on any given announcement has been extracting enormous profits from the swings.

A war doesn't have to be secretly designed to be exploited. It only has to be managed.

## Where This Goes

My read on the next twelve months, stated as projection rather than certainty:

The Strait of Hormuz doesn't fully reopen. Iran's weakened successor government has no incentive to give away its only remaining leverage, and the US has no incentive to remove the condition that makes American energy exports structurally indispensable. A partial closure becomes normalized -- priced in, a new equilibrium.

Oil settles in the $85 to $95 range. The announcement cycle continues, because nothing has emerged to constrain it. The CFTC investigation produces no indictments -- not because the evidence doesn't exist, but because the agency investigating the pattern answers to the person whose announcements created it.

Europe enters a significant recession. They lost cheap Russian gas in 2022 and reliable Gulf oil in 2026. German industrial output contracts. Political instability follows economic pain.

The dollar takes a small but real hit from the yuan-denominated Hormuz tolls Iran is running and from the growing volume of Iran-China oil trade settling outside dollar systems. Not a collapse -- a crack that widens quietly.

Trump claims total victory. Iran's regime is weakened but survives, which turns out to be the ideal outcome for the system as it's currently running: present enough to justify the military posture, too damaged to actually win.

## The Thing I Keep Coming Back To

The volume data from March 22 is public. The congressional letters citing that data are public. The regulatory requests for investigation are public. Multiple news organizations reported on it as it happened.

The trades happened. The timing is documented. The pattern has repeated across multiple events and multiple asset classes going back to at least April 2025.

The only thing standing between a documented suspicious trading pattern and accountability is an agency that answers to the person whose announcements created the pattern.

That is not a conspiracy theory. It is a structural conflict of interest that is operating in plain sight, and has been since the spring.

Based on everything that has happened so far, it appears to be working exactly as designed.
